## Moritz Hardt

{{< keynote_image src="moritz_hardt.jpg" name="Moritz Hardt" affiliation="Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems, Tübingen, Germany" link="https://mrtz.org/" >}}

*Keynote 1: Thursday, November 5, 17:00–18:00*

### Morgenstern's Prophecy and the Future of Prediction

*Abstract:* A century ago, economist Oskar Morgenstern conjectured that predicting the social world would be largely impossible for a fundamental reason: predictions change the very reality they seek to anticipate, a phenomenon we now call performativity. Recent advances in performative prediction challenge Morgenstern’s prophecy, but stop short of refuting it. Existing theory largely rests on the assumption that predictions have only a small influence on outcomes. But what if they don’t? A new principle overcomes this limitation. Repeated model training and deployment recover stable signals in the system, even under arbitrarily large performative effects. The stable signals principle sheds light on what to expect from powerful feedback loops on digital platforms and in training large language models.

*Biography:* Moritz Hardt is a director at the [Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems](https://is.mpg.de/sf/person/mhardt), an honorary professor at the University of Tübingen, and co-founder of grubel. Prior to joining the institute, he was an associate professor in EECS at UC Berkeley and a researcher at Google Brain. Hardt's research contributes to the scientific foundations of artificial intelligence with a focus on social questions. He is the author of *The Emerging Science of Machine Learning Benchmarks* and co-author of *Fairness and Machine Learning* and *Patterns, Predictions, and Actions*.

## Mohsen Bayati

{{< keynote_image src="mohsen_bayati.jpg" name="Mohsen Bayati" affiliation="Stanford Graduate School of Business, USA" link="https://web.stanford.edu/~bayati/" >}}

*Keynote 2: Friday, November 6, 16:30–17:30*

### From Automating Expertise to Scaling Oversight

*Abstract:* As AI agents capably complete tasks that used to require substantial human expertise, a new challenge emerges: determining when human oversight remains necessary. We begin with a clinical prediction problem where extracting meaningful information from unstructured medical notes requires extensive expert effort. We show how multi-agent language models can automate this process while achieving comparable predictive performance at a fraction of the human effort. We then turn to a broader question: when should autonomous agents act independently, and when should they seek human intervention? Building on game-theoretic and statistical frameworks, we explore how agents and overseers can coordinate their decisions. We also discuss how effective oversight can be maintained as AI capabilities continue to advance.

*Biography:* Mohsen Bayati is the Carl and Marilynn Thoma Professor of Operations, Information and Technology at the [Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://web.stanford.edu/~bayati/bio.html). He works on the mathematics of deploying AI in high-stakes settings: alignment and human oversight, experiment design under interference, and sequential decision-making. His research has appeared in *Nature Medicine*, *PNAS*, *Management Science*, *Operations Research* and *NeurIPS*, and has been recognized with the INFORMS Pierskalla and Applied Probability Society best paper awards and an NSF CAREER award.
